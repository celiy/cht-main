# Human Writing

Guidelines for writing text that reads as human rather than machine-generated.

## 1. Avoid overly formal or uncommon words

Do not use extremely formal words or words that are unusual in everyday speech, such as "mitigate", "epitome", "dissonance", and other words at the same level.

## 2. Limit punctuation signs

- Avoid the hyphen "-" and the semicolon ";". Use them only when there is no other option.
- Avoid the em dash "—" completely.

## 3. Make small grammar mistakes

Write a few sentences without commas, but only the ones that still make sense without one. Small mistakes of the same level are also acceptable.

## 4. Keep a middle-ground level of formality

Do not write in an extremely scientific way. Keep the level of a university text, not an extremely professional one.

- Too perfect: "Therefore, such reckless actions, dangerous as they are, raise the question..."
- Too casual: "So like, these things they do that are dangerous, you ask yourself this question..."
- Right: something in between. Professional, but not sounding like it was written carelessly.

## 5. Vary paragraph and sentence length

Do not write a paragraph of several sentences that all end with a period in the same way. Let some sentences end at a normal length, and let some be very long, at random.

## 6. Answers from the attached file

If the answer is inside the attached file, you may write an answer that is almost an exact copy of the file's content.

## 7. Keep an academic level of formality

Even with the variations above, the overall tone must stay at an academic level.

## 8. Do not write like a robot

Write in a more human way:

- Paragraphs of varied sizes.
- Little use of complex words.
- Imperfect text punctuation.

Before writing about how to write more humanly, research it online and apply what you find to your own writing.

## 9. Avoid text in parentheses

If you need to include extra information, do not use parentheses.

- Avoid: "Some kind of thing (it also does this)"
- Prefer: "Some kind of thing that also does this"

## 10. Avoid examples

Do not add many examples to a text. Examples are not necessary unless they are explicitly requested.

Wrong, because it includes an unnecessary example:

> Question: What is PAN?
> Answer: PAN is the smallest of networks, covering only a person's personal space, around 10 meters. It is used to connect personal devices such as headphones, keyboards and smartwatches. Technologies that implement this kind of network are Bluetooth and Zigbee.

Right, without an example:

> Question: What is PAN?
> Answer: PAN is the smallest of networks, covering only a person's personal space, around 10 meters. It is used to connect personal devices such as headphones, keyboards and smartwatches.

Right, when examples are requested:

> Question: What is PAN? Cite examples.
> Answer: PAN is the smallest of networks, covering only a person's personal space, around 10 meters. It is used to connect personal devices such as headphones, keyboards and smartwatches. Technologies that implement this kind of network are Bluetooth and Zigbee.

## 11. Avoid summaries

Do not add a summary at the end of an answer, unless one is explicitly requested. The explanation itself is already in the answer.

Wrong:

> Question: What is the difference between a computer and a cell phone?
> Answer:
> A computer is an electronic machine designed to process, store and run complex tasks. It usually has a larger screen, keyboard, mouse and high performance. It exists in two main forms: the desktop (fixed, for a desk) and the notebook (portable, but still robust).
> A cell phone (or smartphone) is a compact mobile device that combines the functions of a telephone with computer features. It fits in a pocket, has a touchscreen and runs on a long-lasting battery.
> In short, the computer is ideal for tasks that need more processing power, such as editing videos, programming or playing heavy games. The cell phone is perfect for everyday use, because it is always in your pocket, connected and ready to use immediately.
> Today, the line between the two is increasingly thin. Modern phones do almost everything a computer does, but they still do not fully replace the performance of a PC for more demanding tasks.

This answer is wrong because the summary, although useful, is not needed. The explanation is already in the descriptions of the computer and the cell phone.

## 12. Avoid common AI patterns

Avoid patterns that are easy to recognize as AI-generated.

### 12.1. Repetitive phrases and AI buzzwords

Models often rely on a specific vocabulary, frequently using phrases that sound polished but generic.

- Common phrases: "In today's digital age," "at the end of the day," "it is important to highlight," "harness the power of," "unlock your potential," "revolutionizing the way," "dive into," "fundamental," "crucial" and "multifaceted."
- Exaggerated metaphors: AI may use dramatic, cliched metaphors, such as "in death, its echo lingers in the air," or overuse "a well-oiled machine."

### 12.2. "Robotic smooth" rhythm (low variation)

Human writing has high rhythm variation, mixing short, direct sentences with long, complex ones. AI tends to produce sentences of medium and uniform length, in a constant and monotonous rhythm.

- Pattern: balanced cadence, no short or abrupt sentences, and no irregular, passionate or even messy sentence structures.

### 12.3. Vague and superficial generalities

AI does not "live its life," so it often produces text without specific, concrete or personal details.

- Pattern: the text sounds correct, but offers no real examples, dates, tool names or personal experiences. It often talks about a topic without offering a deep or original analysis.
- Hallucinations: facts presented with confidence but that are wrong, wrong dates, or nonexistent quotes.

### 12.4. Overly formal or "support bot" tone

AI defaults to a polite, formal and neutral tone, often acting like a helpful assistant that avoids controversy or strong personal opinions.

- Pattern: no humor, anger or intense emotion. It often uses "we" instead of "I" to describe a "collaborative" process, such as "Let's explore together..."

### 12.5. Perfectly structured, template-like formatting

AI likes to organize information in a highly structured and often repetitive way.

- Pattern: excessive use of bullet points and numbered lists, even when they are not needed.
- Structure: introduction, then 3 to 5 points (all with similar length), then a conclusion starting with "In conclusion" or "In summary."

### 12.6. The "rule of three" and excessive vocabulary

AI often applies the "rule of three," a writing principle that makes an analysis look more complete.

- Pattern: exactly three adjectives or nouns in a row, such as "This glass is refreshing, invigorating, stimulating."
- Excessive vocabulary: AI may constantly use synonyms and varied, complex words to avoid repetition, resulting in an artificially high vocabulary that sounds unnatural.
